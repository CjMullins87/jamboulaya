
# JAMBOULAYA, cabin cooking method

No modifications to the standard recipe are required, however there are substantial changes to method due to the scale in service.

## Required Equipment

* One large pot for browning the sausage and cooking down the veggies
* One large pot for pre-heating the required amount of water
* One large food processor
* Two large, oven-safe chafing dishes
* Multiple large containers for prep, along with colored tape + sharpie for labeling
* At least one good, sharp knife
* Aluminum foil to cover the chafing dishes
* A heat safe, glass measuring cup
* A whole roll of paper towel
* Prep gloves

## Method
### Prep work phase
* Coin the sausage. Set aside into a prep bowl.
	> NOTE:  cutting them on a bias will give you more surface area for browning, so I'd recommend doing so
* Dice the green onion. Set aside into a prep bowl.
* Use the food processor to dice the yellow onion, bell pepper, and celery. Set aside into a prep bowl. This is **the trinity**.
* Use the food processor to dice the garlic, set it aside in a separate prep bowl.
    > NOTE:  We want to avoid burning the garlic, so it gets special treatment
* Pat chicken thighs dry, leave skin on 
    > NOTE:  By the time we went to cook last year, the chicken thighs still had not thawed out. This meant we had to open the package and defrost with cool running water, requiring a lot of paper towels.
* Based on the number of box mixes to be used, measure out the correct amount of water and put it into a pot on the stove. You will want to slowly bring this up to a simmer so that by the time all the ingredients are ready for the oven, you have the water at the appropriate temperature in the appropriate quantity. This is typically going to be a target range of 180-190F.
    > NOTE:  During the first year, we brought it to a boil and held it there, and by the time we were ready for it about a quarter of the water had evaporated. I'd recommend doing this low and slow at first, but accelerating once you are cooking down the trinity

* Set the ovens to 350F.

### Stovetop Cooking Phase

1. Place a large pot with a heavy bottom on the stove and set the heat to medium-high.
2. Get another prep bowl ready for the **browned sausage**
3. Lightly coat the bottom of the pan with a little oil
4. Brown the coined sausage in small batches to avoid *steaming* the sausage instead of browning it. As you cook, you'll want to routinely remove sausage to the **browned sausage** prep bin, and replace it with the yet-to-brown sausage. Once all the sausage is browned, you should have a good layer of fond on the bottom of the pot with no burnt bits.
5. Reduce heat to medium.
6. Add in the diced trinity and repeatedly scrape the bottom of the pot with a spatula. As the trinity releases water, it will deglaze the fond.
7. Once all the fond is picked up, you can add in your cajun seasoning mix. You can gently cook this with the trinity to help build flavor and encourage the trinity to release water faster. You will want to stir consistently.
8. At this point, I would check the reserve pot of water and increase the temperature if it is not yet at a gentle simmer (180-190F). Ensure the required volume is at least approximately correct.
9. After about 15-20 minutes of cooking and regular stirring, the trinity should be significantly reduced in volume and the onions should be translucent. At this stage, add the garlic.
10. Cook gently until the garlic is fragrant, then kill the heat and stir back in the **browned sausage**. Since this has been resting at room temp, it will further drop the temperature  to ensure that the garlic does not burn with carry-over cooking.

### Oven Cooking (Braising) Phase

1. At this point you should have your cooked, seasoned trinity; your browned sausage; and your at-temperature water.
2. Set out the right number of oven-safe chafing dishes. 
3. To each chafing dish, add the appropriate amount of box mix. Last time we prepped for a service of 36, we used 2 dishes and ~3.5 boxes per dish comfortably.
4. Evenly distribute the trinity + sausage among the chafing dishes using a large slotted spoon.
5. Evenly distribute the bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs among the chafing dishes.
6. Finally, evenly distribute the right amount of gently simmering water using an available heat-tolerant measuring cup. You want to be reasonably precise here (within a quarter cup) - adding too much water will mean the jambalya will have to overcook; adding too little water means the jamabalaya will have to undercook.
7. At this point, you can loosely cover the dishes with aluminum foil and transfer them to the oven for at least 40 minutes.
8. At the 40 minute mark, you can remove the aluminum foil and continue to cook until the rice has absorbed all or most of the gravy. Set the foil aside.
9. Once the rice has absorbed all/most of the gravy, remove the chafing dishes to the stove top for holding.

### Finishing Phase

1. Remove the whole chicken thighs to a prep container and then cover the chafing dishes with foil to hold temperature.
2. After the thighs have had at least 10-15 minutes to rest at room temperature,  remove the skins. These will be soggy and gross -- so they are not for eating -- but they will have rendered a lot of fat and flavor into the jambalaya.
3. Using your hands or tools of your choice, remove the meat from the bone and set aside. Due to the braise, the chicken thighs should fall apart easily. I recommend breaking the thighs into large shards so that the meat is clearly visible in the final product.
4. Finally, you can add the braised thigh meat back into the chafing dishes, along with the fresh green onion, and fold to combine.
5. Once combined, the jambalaya is **ready to serve**.

## FAQ / Troubleshooting

> My jambalaya tastes burnt. What did I do wrong?

During the browning phase, you likely burnt either the sausage or the resulting fond. Next time, use a lower heat or more frequent stirring and replacing.

> My chicken thighs will not separate by hand.

This usually means the braise did not happen at the correct temperature for the correct amount of time. Collagen doesn't break down until about 185F, and it takes a while for collagen to fully render - for chicken thighs, typically a 45 minute braise is sufficient.

> What can I do during that 45-60 minute downtime where food is in the oven?

This is where you can make a lot of progress with cleaning. We were also able to make a lot of progress with cleaning while the trinity was cooking down, since it can take 15, 20, 25 minutes depending on the level of heat being applied.
